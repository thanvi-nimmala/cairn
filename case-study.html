(001) The problem

The bank stops your payment. It never tells you what it saw.

You learn two things when a card gets declined: that it was declined, and that you should call someone. The suspicion is real, the reasoning is sealed, and the cost lands on you — a dead card at a counter, twenty minutes on hold, a dispute form that asks you to prove a negative.

The original TruePay concept had the right ingredients — a security score, live alerts, biometric approval, a model watching every transaction. But it spent them on reassurance: badges telling you that you were safe. That is the same black box in nicer packaging. It asks for trust instead of earning it.

(002) The question

Security you never see is invisible. Security you always see is a checkpoint.

This is the tension the whole product sits on, and I did not want to settle it by argument. So I built visibility as a live variable with three settings and made every screen answer to it. You change the posture and look, instead of debating it in a doc.

(003) Reading the risk

People need to know how bad it is before they need to know why.

A held payment normally arrives as a wall of text. But the first question is never why — it is how bad. So the review screen leads with the shape of the suspicion and only then explains itself.

Risk radar Built Unvalidated

Five signals on five axes. You read the silhouette in under a second: a shape pulled hard toward two axes means something specific is wrong, not that everything is faintly off. Confidence sits as one number in the middle.

Then the list appears, strongest-first, each signal's weight drawn as a bar. The radar answers how much; the list answers which. Splitting those two jobs is what stops the screen reading as model output dumped on a user.

(004) Ambient state

A badge competes for attention. Atmosphere does not.

The more badges you add, the less any one of them means. So behind the entire app sits a drifting field of light, drawn on a canvas and blurred, and its colour is bound to security state. You register the change before you read a word.

Colour is never alone Needs testing

The obvious risk with ambient signalling is that colour alone excludes anyone who cannot see it. So the field never carries state by itself — every mood is paired with text, an icon and a semantic token. The field is what makes those three read as one event instead of three notifications.

Paired, yes. But not yet tested with a screen reader — see (009).

(005) The score

A number you cannot move is decoration.

Trust scores in finance apps are usually handed down, with no visible relationship to anything you control. Here the score is the sum of six protection weights. Switch one off and it recalculates in front of you.

Reel 04  Protections come off, then back on.

Why not a ring Built

The visual had to be able to degrade, which ruled out a progress ring — a ring at 42% looks like a ring at 96%, only shorter. Instead the score is 72 ticks riding a standing wave. Lit ticks encode the value; a second, rougher harmonic scales with how much cover you have lost.

So a weakened score does not merely shrink. It visibly frays. The number and the feeling move together.

(006) The decision

Held is not blocked. The model narrows the choice; it never takes it.

There is no path where the review screen resolves itself and tells you afterwards. Both outcomes sit in a footer pinned to the bottom of the sheet, so however long the signal list runs, the decision stays one thumb-reach away.

Still  Scrolled past every reason to doubt — the decision has not moved.

A fix, not a plan Fixed in build

The first version let the buttons sit at the end of the content. On a 390-point screen the signal list pushed them below the fold: the single most important control in the product, reachable only by scrolling past the reasons to distrust it. Sticky footer, gradient fade, problem gone.

Both outcomes are honest about consequence. Yes, this was me releases the payment and folds the merchant into what the model trusts. Not me freezes the card, opens a dispute, and says plainly that you are not liable while it is reviewed. Neither is a dead end.

I only caught this because the prototype was real enough to scroll.

(007) The craft

Dark fintech has a house style. I went to banknotes instead.

Following the house style would have produced a competent app nobody remembers. The material I reached for was security printing — banknotes, share certificates, cheque stock — not as pastiche, but for the two things it does well: fine line-work that signals value, and typography that treats numbers as the subject.

Guilloché Built

Real spirograph geometry sits under the balance and every card face — the same construction used on currency to make forgery expensive. It is drawn faintly enough that most people will never consciously notice it, and that is the point: it makes those surfaces read as an object with value rather than a coloured rectangle. The sign at the top of this page is the same routine, turned up.

(008) The system

Every colour and corner in the build is one token away from changing.

The prototype ships with its own control panel. Accent, corner radius, type pairing and AI presence are all live, and each one writes a CSS custom property the whole app reads — no component holds a literal colour or radius. It exists so a design review can change the argument mid-sentence instead of asking for another round of comps.

(009) What's next

What the prototype has not earned yet.

A shell proves a direction, not a product. These are the five things I would put in front of people before calling any of it settled.

  • Does the radar actually read?Untested

    I believe the silhouette is faster than a list. That is a hypothesis, and it needs five people and a stopwatch, not my confidence.

  • An accessibility pass on the ambient fieldUntested

    State is carried by colour, icon and text together — but I have not tested it with a screen reader, or at reduced motion beyond honouring the media query.

  • The watching verdict needs a homeNext build

    Recurring subscriptions sit between cleared and held, and right now that middle verdict only appears as a badge. It deserves a surface of its own.

  • Business mode is a switch, not a flowNext build

    Dual approval over a threshold is stated in the profile and implemented nowhere. It is the obvious next thing to build.

  • Error and empty statesNext build

    The shell shows the happy path plus one hold. A real product fails in ten more ways, and those screens are where trust is actually won or lost.
